# Top-down pixel art, in code

Every image in this game is generated by code at load time: no PNGs. That keeps the style consistent and makes
any asset easy to recolor, vary by seed, or regenerate. The method is pixelfx's (see `vendor/pixelfx`): **code is
clay, trust your eyes** — draft, render, look, fix the worst thing, render again. Three rounds minimum.

## The style bible

- **Camera**: high angle, 3/4 top-down. Ground is seen from above; anything standing shows its *top* and its
  *front*. Walls: a light top face (~10 px) over a darker front face. Round things are ellipses squashed to ~0.8.
- **Scale**: tiles are 32 px. A human is ~30 px tall with a big head (head ≈ 12 px, chibi proportions); goblins
  ~24 px. Trees are 100+ px tall and tower over the party.
- **Light**: one sun, top-left. Bright step on top-left edges, dark step on bottom-right. Shadows are soft
  dithered ellipses under everything that stands (`makeShadow`).
- **Color**: saturated and warm. Hard ramp steps, never smooth gradients; blend between steps only with the 4×4
  Bayer dither (`rampAt`). Ramps live in `src/art/ground.js` (`PAL`) and `src/art/props.js` (`RAMP`).
  Grass greens, ochre meadows, turquoise water, orange wood, maroon/purple bark, mossy grey stone.
- **Ink**: every sprite gets a 1 px dark outline `[24,18,30]` (`PixBuf.outline`). The ground does not.
- **Texture**: small two-tone marks (grass blades, leaf specks, bark furrows, plank grain, knots). Detail at the
  1 px level, but never lone noise pixels inside a flat shape.
- **Life**: things move a little — grass sways (4 frames), water glints, idle characters breathe 1 px.
- **Mood by area**: meadows are bright; the ruins get a cool dark tint and mist. Do it with overlays in
  `draw()`, not by repainting the art.
- Inspired by the look of lush top-down pixel RPGs; **never copy a specific game's sprites, characters, names or
  logos**. Generic motifs are fine.

## Where things are

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `src/util.js` | `PixBuf` (set/outline/canvas), `rampAt` (dithered ramp lookup), `fbm`/`vnoise`/`hash2`, `Rng` |
| `src/art/ground.js` | the whole map painted per pixel: grass, dry meadow, road, flagstones, water, lily pads, dock |
| `src/art/props.js` | `spheres()` (shaded blob clusters: canopies, rocks, bushes), trees, pines, dead trees, walls, pillars, camp props |
| `src/art/chars.js` | `SPECS` (palette + parts per character) and `buildCharacter(spec)` → 4 dirs × [walk0-3, idle, attack, cast] |
| `src/world.js` | map layout: where water, roads, ruins, trees and props go |
| `src/fx.js` | pixelfx bridge: effects in world coordinates, composited color + additive light |
| `tools/sheet.html` | contact sheet of every asset at 3×: `http://localhost:5173/tools/sheet.html` (`?only=chars` / `?only=props`) |

## Make a new asset

1. **Say it in one line** with art direction: "a mossy stone well: round top rim seen from above, dark water
   inside, bucket on a rope, moss dripping on the front".
2. **Start from the closest generator** in `props.js` or `chars.js` and copy its structure. Build with
   `PixBuf`, shade with `rampAt` against a ramp, finish with `pb.outline(INK)`, return
   `{ c: pb.canvas(), ox, oy, shadow }` where `(ox, oy)` is the anchor at the feet/base (that's what depth sorting uses).
3. **Add it to `tools/sheet.html`** and open the sheet in the browser pane. Screenshot it and judge it next to its
   neighbours, at 3×. Then put it in the world (`world.js` → `put({ kind, x, y }, blockedTiles)` and the `propArt`
   / `makeArt` switch in `main.js`) and look at it in the game at 2×.
4. **Fix the worst thing, repeat**:

| you see | fix |
|---|---|
| reads as mush | fewer ramp steps, a clear silhouette, bigger shapes, one focal point |
| flat | stronger top-left highlight, a darker bottom-right/base step |
| looks like a wall when it should be a floor | top-down floors are irregular cells (see `flagstone`, Voronoi), not brick courses |
| "turtle" blobs | small domes with an outline read as shells: add leaf specks and an irregular edge, or make it bigger |
| floats | missing shadow, or the anchor `oy` isn't at the base |
| clashes | it's using colors off the ramps; stay on `PAL`/`RAMP`, or add a new ramp that matches their value range |
| too smooth / vector | remove gradients, quantize with `rampAt`, dither only between neighbouring steps |

### Characters

Add a spec to `SPECS` in `chars.js`: `skin`, `hair` + `hairStyle` (`short long bun hood mohawk bald`), `tunic`,
`pants`, `boots`, optional `cape`, `robe`, `apron`, `trim`, `weapon` (`sword staff dagger axe none`), `orb` (staff
gem), `ears` (`human elf goblin`), `eyes`, `small`/`big`. Check all 4 facings and the walk cycle on the sheet:
legs alternate, arms swing opposite, the body bobs 1 px on contact frames.

## pixelfx effects on a top-down map

The pixelfx library is side-view, but most effects read fine from above if you:

- anchor at the **feet** (`y = unit.y`) for ground effects and at the **chest** (`y - 14`) for hits;
- shrink to our scale (`radius` ~24 for `melee/sword-slash`, `radius: 4` for `fire/fireball`);
- face left with `flip`, aim projectiles with `angle` = `atan2(dy, dx)` and move them with `FxLayer.spawn(..., { move })`;
- recolor with `palette` (`PAL.fire`, `PAL.arcane`, `PAL.holy`, `PAL.heal`...).

Avoid: effects with `actor: 2` unless you pass our own sprite as `o.actor` (otherwise they paint the stock dummy),
and the 320×180 `weather/*` loops (they draw their own backdrop and show as a box). Ambient life (fireflies,
butterflies) is drawn directly in `drawCritters()` instead. Use the pixelfx skill's own render/check loop
(`vendor/pixelfx` has the tools in the upstream repo) when authoring a brand-new effect.
